The multilingual BERT model demonstrated a meaningful ability to capture semantic similarity between English and Arabic climate-related texts. Several English-Arabic text pairs discussing similar climate topics produced relatively high cosine similarity scores. For example, the English text about the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report and the Arabic text discussing the IPCC report achieved a similarity score of 0.7375. Additionally, the English text about COP28 agreements showed stronger similarity with the Arabic COP28-related article than with unrelated Arabic texts. These results suggest that multilingual BERT can partially align semantically related concepts across languages within a shared embedding space.

However, the similarity scores also showed limitations in cross-lingual understanding. Some unrelated English-Arabic pairs still received moderately high similarity scores, indicating that the multilingual embedding space is not perfectly separated by topic. Cross-lingual similarities were generally lower and less precise than typical within-language semantic similarities. Despite this limitation, the heatmap visualization revealed visible clustering patterns between climate-related English and Arabic texts. This demonstrates that multilingual embeddings could support bilingual semantic search, retrieval systems, and recommendation systems in the MENA region without requiring completely separate NLP pipelines for Arabic and English.
